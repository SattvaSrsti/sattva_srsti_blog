Why is my Mysore masala dosa not crispy?

Crispy dosas are a delight, but they can be tricky.

65 minMediumSpice: medium1 serving
Cook & customize on SattvaSrsti

What happened

Imagine preparing a Mysore masala dosa, excited for that perfect crisp. But when you flip it, itΓÇÖs soft and sad instead of golden and crunchy.

The short answer

To achieve a crispy Mysore masala dosa, ensure your batter is flowyΓÇönot watery or thick. A well-heated iron tawa is crucial; sprinkle water to check if it sizzles before spreading the batter. Oil around the edges helps with crispness, but too much oil can make it greasy. Remember, a good Mysore dosa is crisp outside and slightly soft inside, not a paper-thin roast.

Why this dish is cooked this way

Mysore Masala Dosa is a crisp fermented rice-lentil crepe spread with spicy red chutney and filled with potato masala. A Bengaluru and Mysuru tiffin classic, it is popular for breakfast, brunch, and festive South Indian meals.

  1. Prep

    Rinse Sona Masuri rice, chana dal, and fenugreek seed until the water runs mostly clear, then soak them in fresh water.

  2. Prep

    Rinse urad dal separately until the water is mostly clear, then soak it in fresh water so it softens evenly for grinding.

  3. Prep

    Rinse poha briefly, then soak it separately in water just until soft. Drain it before grinding so the batter does not become watery.

Related problems

Real questions people ask ΓÇö answered the SattvaSrsti way.

What makes Mysore masala dosa different from regular masala dosa?

The difference is the fiery red chilli-garlic chutney smeared inside before the potato filling.

Why does my dosa stick to the pan?

Pan neither too hot nor too cold ΓÇö water should sizzle, not explode into steam.

Should dosa batter be thick or thin?

For crisp dosa, batter should pour like thin cream / thin pancake batter ΓÇö spreadable, not stiff.

Why does the potato filling make my dosa soggy?

Add potato masala only at the end, once the dosa has set.

Storage

  • Store leftover batter, potato filling, and red chutney in separate airtight containers in the refrigerator.
  • Reheat potato filling in a pan with a splash of water; bring batter to room temperature, stir gently, and make fresh dosas on a hot pan.
  • Use fermented batter within 3 days, potato filling within 2 days, and red chutney within 4 days.
